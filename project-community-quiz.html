Community Cultural In-Between Quiz

A reflective identity quiz about navigating cultural in-betweenness—how identity can shift across language, place, behavior, and belonging.

Design Process & Reflection

This project allowed me to explore community through the lens of cultural in-betweenness, especially the experience of moving between languages, places, behaviors, and expectations. Instead of treating identity as something fixed, I wanted the quiz to feel more reflective and fluid, where the result is not a strict label but a way for the user to recognize certain patterns in themselves.

I chose a soft, atmospheric visual style to match the emotional tone of the project. Since the quiz deals with belonging, displacement, and self-recognition, I wanted the interface to feel personal and slightly dreamlike rather than clinical or overly direct. The archetypes helped me organize different relationships to cultural identity while still allowing overlap between them.

Through this project, I learned how quizzes can be used as more than entertainment. They can become a form of guided reflection, especially when the questions are connected to lived experience. At the same time, I also realized that the current structure still follows a fairly standard quiz format. While the concept and visual direction feel personal to my community and topic, the interaction itself could be pushed further to feel more original and less template-based.

Next Steps

If I continued developing this project, I would move away from the generic question-by-question quiz structure and redesign the interaction to feel more like a personal journey through cultural memory. Instead of only selecting answers from a list, users could move through different visual scenes related to language, home, food, social behavior, or family expectations. Each choice could subtly change the atmosphere, color, layout, or text on the screen, making the experience feel more responsive and custom.

I would also like to include more open-ended questions where users can type their own answers instead of only choosing from multiple-choice options. This would allow the quiz to ask more personal and specific questions, and would make the experience feel less fixed. Since the project is about identity and belonging, written responses could better capture small details that multiple choice answers often miss, like a phrase someone grew up hearing, a food they associate with home, or a moment when they felt between cultures.

Another next step would be to make the results less like a final category and more like a layered identity portrait. Rather than giving users one fixed archetype, the final screen could show a mix of influences, almost like a cultural map or collage. This would better reflect the idea that people living between cultures often do not fit into one clear box.

I would also want to make the design more connected to my own visual language. I could create custom illustrations, handwritten elements, animated transitions, or archival textures that feel less like a generated quiz interface and more like a designed experience. Overall, I see this version as a strong starting point, but the next stage would focus on making the interaction itself as personal and nuanced as the topic.
