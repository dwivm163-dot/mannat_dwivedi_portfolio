GENERATIVE ART 008

Interactive Typography

Exploring the intersection of type and interactivity through three expressive p5.js experiments.

01

Toxic

Prototype Video
Live Sketch ↗

Toxic treats the word as something corrosive: the letterforms do not sit still on the page, they seethe, drip, and deform as if the surface itself is being eaten away. I leaned into acidic greens and yellows against deep, murky contrast so the palette reads as chemical and unsafe rather than decorative. The motion is restless and slightly unpredictable, which mirrors how poison feels—fine until it is not—and the interaction lets the viewer disturb the type so harm spreads from contact, tying legibility directly to the idea of contamination.

02

Bitten

Prototype Video
Live Sketch ↗

Bitten is about interruption: a clean word interrupted by missing chunks and jagged edges, as if something has torn into the letterforms. The palette stays relatively restrained so the “damage” reads clearly—warm flesh tones or sharp contrast where the bite happens—while motion emphasizes sudden loss rather than smooth flow. Interaction becomes the act of taking another bite: each gesture removes or displaces part of the word, so meaning and readability literally diminish as the concept plays out.

03

Burnout

Prototype Video
Live Sketch ↗

Burnout is exhaustion made visible: the type thins, flickers, or collapses under its own weight instead of holding a crisp silhouette. I used drained, ashy color shifts and slow, heavy motion so the word feels like it is running out of energy rather than exploding. Where interaction appears, it often asks for effort that yields diminishing returns—mirroring how burnout accumulates in small repeated costs until the whole system feels hollow.

reflection

Process & Takeaways

Working across three single-word prompts pushed me to treat typography as performance, not just layout. Each sketch had to answer a narrow question: what does this word feel like in color, motion, and touch, and where can I sacrifice perfect legibility for a clearer emotional read?

p5.js made it easy to iterate on behavior quickly, but it also tempted me to add effects for their own sake. The most successful moments were the ones where interaction had a consequence—spreading damage, taking another bite, or wearing the letters down—so the viewer’s action and the word’s meaning stayed locked together.

If I continued the series, I would prototype more slowly with paper thumbnails first, then translate only the strongest gestures into code. I would also test on different screens earlier; motion-heavy type reads very differently at phone scale than on a large monitor.
