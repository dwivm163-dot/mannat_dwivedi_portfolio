GENERATIVE ART 008

Interactive Typography

Exploring the intersection of type and interactivity through three expressive p5.js experiments.

01

Toxic

Prototype Video
Live Sketch ↗

For Toxic, I wanted the word to feel unstable and hazardous, as if it were reacting chemically on screen. I picked acidic greens and neons because they immediately signal danger, toxins, and warning signs. The black background makes the colors appear more striking and a little alarming.

Visually, I approached the letters like they were polluted surfaces, rather than set typography. The type distorts, moves, and appears to be breaking down with time. The interaction enhances this concept by having the viewer's movement trigger the word, as if touching it spreads the toxicity. The idea was for the word to not just say "toxic," but to act like toxic substance.

02

Bitten

Prototype Video
Live Sketch ↗

For Bitten, I wanted the design to be vivid, disturbing, and slightly gory, thus I drew the design around the image of a monster-like mouth biting down and drawing blood. I selected the word because it connotes abrupt pain, force, and physical action, and I wanted the interaction to convey this sensation . In this sketch, the cursor mimics the user's fingertip, and as it travels close to the mouth, the fangs bite down, as if the viewer is placing their own finger in danger. This makes the interaction seem more personal and reactive.

I purposely selected red to emphasize blood and injury, and had the pouring blood spell out the phrase "bitten" so that the typography became part of the scene rather than separate. 

03

Burnout

Prototype Video
Live Sketch ↗

For Burnout, I intended for the interaction to feel slower and more gradual compared to the other two pieces, since burnout generally never happens abruptly. Unlike Toxic and Bitten, which are more immediate and abrupt, this one was designed to build up over time. I related the concept of emotional and mental burnout due to overwhelming stress with the image of a lightbulb going out. It felt like a powerful metaphor for gradually wearing oneself down until you can no longer go on.

In the interaction, the user flips a light switch, which illuminates a hanging bulb. As the light turns on, it reveals the word that had previously been masked in the darkness. The light from the bulb gradually  intensifies, reflecting increasing tension and pressure. Eventually, the light begins to flicker, indicating instability and lack of energy before the bulb bursts. I utilized this scenario to demonstrate burnout as a steady depletion rather than a single dramatic event. Overall, the interaction, tempo, and visuals were all designed in order to convey the feeling of mounting pressure, which finally leads to collapse.

reflection

Process & Takeaways

This assignment helped me think about typography as something that can behave, not just something that can be read. For each word, I tried to make the interaction feel connected to the meaning instead of just adding motion for the sake of it. Toxic became about spreading and contamination, Bitten became about a sudden physical reaction, and Burnout became about a slower buildup toward collapse.

I also realized how much timing changes the feeling of an interaction. The faster, sharper response in Bitten made it feel more violent and immediate, while the slow glow and flicker in Burnout made the word feel more tense and draining. Thinking through color, motion, and user input together helped me understand how interactive typography can create a small experience around a word, rather than simply illustrate it.

If I continued developing this project, I would refine the transitions and animations so each interaction feels more polished. I would also add audio to make the pieces more immersive. For Toxic, I imagined a bubbling or boiling sound for the green substance; for Bitten, a biting sound effect or scream; and for Burnout, a sharp shattering sound when the lightbulb explodes. I was not able to add these sound elements within the time I had, but they would make the interactions feel more complete and physically responsive.
